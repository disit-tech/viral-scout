# Originality and duplicate detection

How Instagram, TikTok and YouTube spot reposted, duplicate and copyrighted short videos, what happens when they do, and how a small business stays original. Research from November 2026. Every claim links to its source.

- **[snippet-only]**: confirmed from a search result because the page didn't render.
- **[secondary]**: from trade press, not the platform itself.
- **[unverified]**: the page couldn't be opened.

> **The short version.** All three platforms compare uploads against fingerprints of existing video and audio, and they rank down content they judge unoriginal. Their detection is **designed to recognise footage after common edits** (crops, speed changes, overlays, colour changes). Their policies also say **minor edits don't make content original**. The only reliable route is the one viral-scout is built around: **copy the format, never the footage.**

## 1. Platform by platform

### Meta (Instagram and Facebook)

**Rights-holder matching: Rights Manager and Audible Magic**
- Rights Manager "identifies videos on Facebook and Instagram, including Live videos" that match reference files uploaded by rights holders. Meta also uses Audible Magic, which flags uploads "when an audio match is detected". [Meta Transparency Center](https://transparency.meta.com/reports/intellectual-property/protecting-intellectual-property-rights/)
- When a video matches, the rights holder can **block** it (nobody but the uploader sees it), **claim the ad earnings**, **monitor** it, or **report** it. Meta "disable[s] the accounts of repeat infringers in appropriate circumstances". (same page)
- Match rules let the rights holder choose how much of the reference must overlap, whether the video, the audio or both must match, and what happens next. [Meta Business Help](https://en-gb.facebook.com/business/help/475548192814461) [snippet-only]
- The uploader can dispute a match. The rights holder then upholds the block, releases it, or files a takedown. [Meta Business Help](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/212149709664331) [snippet-only]

**Ranking: originality on Instagram**
- Instagram is "less likely to recommend reposts of a reel that's already on Instagram", content with "noticeable watermarks", and "accounts that regularly collect and reshare others' content". It suggests Remix instead, to "add your own spin". [Instagram creators blog, 31 May 2023](https://creators.instagram.com/blog/tips-for-improving-your-reach)
- Content that breaks the Recommendation Guidelines can still reach followers, but it stays out of Explore and Reels for everyone else. **Account Status** shows whether an account can be recommended. (same page)
- Stitching clips together or adding a watermark is not a meaningful edit. [Instagram Help](https://help.instagram.com/1800814370401535/) [snippet-only]
- **April 2024 update** [secondary]:
  - When identical posts exist, only the original is recommended.
  - Reposts get a label linking to the original creator.
  - Aggregator accounts are dropped from recommendations.
  - Adam Mosseri: "We build classifiers to predict how *likely* something is to be original."

  Sources: [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-algorithm-prioritize-original-emerging-creators/714777/), [PetaPixel](https://petapixel.com/2024/04/30/instagrams-new-algorithm-punishes-copycats-rewards-original-creators)
- A 2026 report says the approach now covers photos and carousels too. [PetaPixel](https://petapixel.com/2026/04/30/new-instagram-policies-target-reposted-content/) [unverified]

**Facebook crackdown on unoriginal content (14 July 2025)** [Facebook creators blog](https://creators.facebook.com/blog/combating-unoriginal-content)
- Unoriginal content is content that "reuses or repurposes another creator's content repeatedly without crediting them". "Simply stitching together clips or adding your watermark does not qualify as meaningful enhancement."
- **Repeat offenders:** monetization is removed "for a period of time", and they get "reduced distribution on everything they share".
- **Duplicates:** "if our systems detect duplicate videos… we will reduce the distribution of the copies". Meta is testing links on copies that point back to the original.
- **Welcome:** reaction videos, commentary and joining trends, "as long as you make it your own". Professional Dashboard shows recommendation and monetization risk.

### YouTube

**Content ID**
- Every upload "is automatically scanned by Content ID" against "a database of audio and visual files submitted by copyright owners". [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2797370?hl=en)
- On a match, the owner's settings decide whether the video is **blocked**, **monetized** for the owner (sometimes shared) or **tracked**. The outcome can differ by country. (same page)
- **A claim is not a strike.** Content ID claims are automatic. A **copyright strike** follows a legal removal request: the video is removed, and three strikes can terminate the channel. [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/7002106) · [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2814000?hl=en) [snippet-only]

**Reused content policy (YouTube Partner Program)** [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)
- **Not monetizable:**
  - compilations "with little or no narrative"
  - "short videos you compiled from other social media websites"
  - content without "substantive modifications"
  - "making minimal changes, and calling it your own original work"
- **Fine:** critical reviews, reactions that comment on the original, and edits that add a storyline and commentary.
- The policy applies **even if you have permission**, and it can remove monetization **from the whole channel**. It's separate from copyright, and it's "not based on… fair use".
- July 2025: "repetitious" content was renamed "inauthentic" content; the reused content policy didn't change. [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/youtube-clarifies-inauthentic-content-policy-changes/) [secondary]

### TikTok

- "You should only post content you created or have the right to share." Content that breaks the guidelines is removed. "Content is also ineligible for the FYF if it includes unoriginal or reused material without anything new." [TikTok Community Guidelines: Integrity and authenticity](https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/integrity-authenticity)
- A video that isn't eligible for the For You feed (FYF) is still visible in search and on the profile. Analytics show when a video was made ineligible. [TikTok FYF standards](https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/fyf-standards)
- **IP policy:**
  - Infringing content "may be removed".
  - Repeat infringers may be banned, and TikTok can ban immediately "in cases of severe copyright violations".
  - Fair use and fair dealing are acknowledged.

  [TikTok copyright policy](https://www.tiktok.com/legal/page/global/copyright-policy/en)
- **Audio:**
  - Sounds that break copyright are removed, sometimes only the infringing section.
  - You can appeal, or swap in a library track.
  - A "Music copyright check" runs before you post.

  [TikTok Support](https://support.tiktok.com/en/log-in-troubleshoot/troubleshooting/a-sound-was-removed-from-my-video) · [Creator Academy](https://www.tiktok.com/creator-academy/article/appeal-muted-video) [snippet-only]
- **TikTok doesn't publish how its matching works.** Press and Audible Magic's own materials list TikTok as an Audible Magic client. [Billboard](https://www.billboard.com/?p=8514899) [secondary, dated, unverified as current]
- The August 2025 guidelines rewrite spread the old "Unoriginal, low-quality, and QR code content" section across other sections. [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/08/15/tiktoks-new-guidelines-add-subtle-changes-for-live-creators-ai-content-and-more/)

### Audible Magic (used across platforms)
- Licensed audio and video fingerprint matching. Its materials name Facebook and Twitch as clients, and it reported more than 3 billion identification requests a month in 2019.
- It "performs no blocking": the platform applies the rights holder's rules.

[Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audible_Magic) · [Audible Magic](https://www.audiblemagic.com/identification/) [snippet-only]

## 2. How the technology works (high level)

| Technique | What it does | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Perceptual image hashing (PDQ)** | Meta's open-source hasher "tries to match images which people perceive as similar". It uses a DCT and gives a quality score; two hashes match at a distance of 31 or less. Its docs mention rotation and flip variants. | [facebook/ThreatExchange PDQ](https://github.com/facebook/ThreatExchange/tree/main/pdq) |
| **Video hashing (TMK+PDQF)** | Open-sourced by Meta in 2019 for matching "identical and nearly identical" videos at scale. | [Meta newsroom, Aug 2019](https://about.fb.com/news/2019/08/open-source-photo-video-matching/) |
| **Audio fingerprinting** | Shazam-style: picks peaks in the spectrogram (a "constellation map") and hashes pairs of peaks by their frequencies and time gap. Robust to noise; searches at about 1000× real time. | [Wang 2003](https://www.princeton.edu/~cuff/ele201/files/Wang03-shazam.pdf) |
| **Learned copy detection** | Models trained to decide whether an image or video is a *modified copy* of a reference. | ISC2021 [arXiv 2106.09672](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.09672), winners [arXiv 2202.04007](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.04007) · VSC2022/23 [arXiv 2306.09489](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.09489), [vsc.drivendata.org](https://vsc.drivendata.org/) |

**What matters for creators:** Meta's Video Similarity Challenge built its test set from copies with cropping, padding, rotation, flipping, aspect-ratio and speed changes, text, emoji and frame overlays, colour changes, blur, stacking, screen-recording simulation and spliced sources. Top systems raised accuracy from 0.60 to 0.87 µAP. In other words, **the research field's benchmark is recognising footage after exactly those edits**. On top of that:
- **Policies say those edits don't count.** Watermarks, borders, stitching and minimal changes are named as not making content original (the Meta 2025 post and the YouTube reused content policy above).
- **Accounts matter, not just files.** Meta lists "prior violations by problematic accounts" as a detection signal, and Instagram ranks down accounts that regularly reshare others' content.

That's why viral-scout has no "remix someone else's video" feature: it would cost an account reach, monetization and possibly the account itself.

## 4. Checklist: staying original

1. **Film your own footage and record your own voice.** Original content gets the most distribution.
2. **Copy the format, not the footage.** Recreating a trend's structure (hook → body → payoff) with your own shots is "joining a trend". Re-uploading someone else's clip is a duplicate. This is what viral-scout's ideas notes and swipe file are for.
3. **Use the platform's own tools** (Remix, Duet, Stitch, Shorts remix) when you build on another video directly. Add real commentary or teaching, not just a caption.
4. **Credit sources.** It's part of Meta's definition. Credit alone doesn't make a repost original.
5. **Use licensed audio.** On business accounts, use the libraries above, not trending commercial songs.
6. **Permission isn't originality on YouTube.** Monetized reused content still needs a meaningful contribution from you.
7. **Repurposing your own videos across platforms is fine.** These policies target reusing *another creator's* content. Remove other apps' visible watermarks before you cross-post.
8. **Check your status:** Instagram Account Status, Facebook Professional Dashboard, TikTok analytics (FYF eligibility), YouTube Studio claims. Dispute only with a valid reason.
